Abstract

Reference-based style transfer with diffusion models aims to synthesize artistic textures while preserving the geometry and semantics of a target image. However, training-free methods face a trade-off between style fidelity and content preservation: strong style injection can distort structure and cause content leakage, while conservative transfer often leads to under-stylization.


We introduce OrthoStyle, a training-free framework that addresses this trade-off without network fine-tuning or DDIM inversion. OrthoStyle consists of three complementary components: Semantic Gating to preserve structural and semantic cues; Score-Orthogonal DINO Guidance to project style gradients orthogonally to the diffusion score direction, preserving the sampling trajectory near the data manifold; and an Early Step Controller that applies AdaIN-based clean-latent pushforward to establish color and layout before fine-grained stylization.


Extensive quantitative and qualitative comparisons with both OC-based baselines and representative stylization methods show that OrthoStyle achieves a favorable balance between style fidelity and content preservation while maintaining structural consistency. Most notably, it completely outperforms the baseline that uses the same Stable Cascade backbone.

Methodology

① Semantic Gating

Structural prior without clutter

Extracts Canny edge maps and automatically removes the smallest 20% of detected edges using salient foreground segmentation masks, preventing background clutter from over-constraining stylization.

Mechanism: Raw Canny edge maps capture noisy background textures that restrict artistic brushstrokes. Semantic Gating focuses structural guidance strictly on the salient subject contours, keeping clean spatial layout while unlocking freedom for style textures.

▾ Hover or click for detailed explanation
② Score-Orthogonal Guidance

Project gradients to tangent manifold

Standard external gradients frequently oppose the diffusion score, destabilizing the trajectory. OrthoStyle projects DINO style gradients orthogonally to the diffusion score direction.

Mechanism: We compute ∇xt Lstyle via Tweedie estimation and subtract its parallel projection along the score vector sθ(xt, t). This minimizes denoising interference, completely eliminating the structural collapse common in unconstrained guidance.

▾ Hover or click for detailed explanation
③ Early-Step Controller

Clean-latent AdaIN pushforward

Applies AdaIN normalization to Tweedie-predicted clean latents x̂0 exclusively during early diffusion timesteps t ∈ [τ, T], establishing global palette and texture distribution.

Mechanism: Diffusion sampling develops global structure in early steps and fine details in late steps. By pushing clean latents toward style statistics during early steps only, OrthoStyle locks in palette harmony without disrupting late-stage geometric rendering.

▾ Hover or click for detailed explanation
④ Blended Style Tokens

Cross-attention leakage suppression

Blends style prompt representations with mean style embeddings with interpolation weight αs = 0.85, preventing reference subject elements from leaking into content.

Mechanism: Raw style token embeddings frequently encode both style texture and the semantic identity of the reference subject. Blending with mean-pooled tokens neutralizes semantic identity while retaining high-fidelity artistic brush dynamics.

b. Pushforward Step Threshold Controllability

We evaluate the effect of varying the clean-latent pushforward threshold τ ∈ {1, 2, 3, 4}. Intervening on Tweedie-predicted latents for additional initial steps progressively strengthens content geometry and suppresses leakage at inference time without network retraining.

Flexible Inference-Time Control: As τ increases from 1 to 4, content preservation (CLIP-I) monotonically increases from 75.90 to 79.40 and semantic leakage (DCL) drops from 27.27 to 21.65. This provides users with an intuitive, zero-training control slider between stylized artistic intensity (τ = 1) and photorealistic structural fidelity (τ = 4).
